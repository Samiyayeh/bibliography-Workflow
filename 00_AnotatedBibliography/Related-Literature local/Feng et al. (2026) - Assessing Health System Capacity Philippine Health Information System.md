# Assessing health system and service delivery capacity in responding to changing disease patterns in the Philippines: an analysis using national health information system longitudinal data

**Category:** [[Related Literature local]]

**Yikai Feng, Yueran Yang, Qiao Wang, Zihan Meng, Ying Deng, and Peilong Liu. 2026. Assessing health system and service delivery capacity in responding to changing disease patterns in the Philippines: an analysis using national health information system longitudinal data. *Human Resources for Health* 24, 1 (January 2026), Article 13. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12960-025-01041-z**

- **Summary:** Analyzing longitudinal health information system datasets from the Philippine Department of Health's Field Health Services Information System (FHSIS) and Philippine Health Statistics, this health systems policy study establishes that primary health delivery units in Philippine local government units face severe operational mismatches between heavy outpatient disease burdens and frontline workforce capacity, underscoring the necessity for automated digital data management to optimize clinic resource allocation and patient intake.
- **Relevance:** The empirical analysis of Philippine public health information systems justifies the operational necessity of the proposed Naga City Health Office system, as documenting that primary health units in Philippine LGUs are overburdened by escalating patient volumes with constrained clinical staff establishes that manual intake and paper records exacerbate service delivery bottlenecks. Specifically, these findings validate the NCHO HIS objective of automating patient queue routing and digitizing clinical history retrieval, directly mitigating workforce overload and expanding patient service capacity in local municipal healthcare facilities.
