# Implementing Electronic Health Records in Philippine Primary Care Settings: Mixed-Methods Pilot Study

**Category:** [[Related Literature local]]

**ACM Citation:**
Elepaño, A., Tan-Lim, C. S., Javelosa, M. A., De Mesa, R. Y., Rey, M., Sanchez, J., Dans, L., and Dans, A. M. 2025. Implementing Electronic Health Records in Philippine Primary Care Settings: Mixed-Methods Pilot Study. *JMIR Medical Informatics* 13 (2025), e63036. https://doi.org/10.2196/63036

- **Summary:**  
  Elepaño et al. (2025) evaluated the longitudinal implementation of two electronic health record platforms across urban, rural, and remote primary care facilities in the Philippines using an explanatory mixed-methods design, finding that user acceptability significantly declined among physicians and administrative staff when transitioning to a less flexible interface despite its privacy-focused design.

- **Relevance:**  
  This empirical literature establishes the frontline operational constraints and interface usability requirements governing public health informatics deployments within Philippine primary care settings. Specifically, the authors' analysis demonstrates that rigid system architectures and high documentation friction directly degrade clinician adoption rates in municipal health facilities. However, the findings confirm that systems incorporating iterative user feedback, cross-platform accessibility, and direct alignment with routine consultation workflows achieve sustained user acceptance. For the proposed Naga City Health Office project, these findings provide the architectural justification for engineering an intuitive, component-based user interface integrated with automated queue routing. Ensuring that the electronic health record module minimizes cognitive load and mirrors local consultation procedures directly supports ISO/IEC 25010 usability benchmarks and prevents implementation resistance among city health workers.

- **Source Reference(s):**  
  - **Section:** *Methods – Study Design and Setting; Systems Deployed* (Details the comparative deployment of OpenMRS-based and Microsoft-based EHR platforms across urban, rural, and remote Philippine primary care facilities from 2020 to 2022).  
  - **Section:** *Results – User Acceptability and Content Analysis* (Documents the 351 survey responses showing the significant decline in behavioral intention to use among urban physicians and administrative personnel following the transition to the more restrictive interface).  
  - **Direct Quote:** *"While the newer system offered a more privacy-focused design, qualitative analysis revealed that users preferred the previous system... implementation strategies must be tailored to the specific needs and capacities of each unique setting."* (Sections: *Abstract*, *Results*, and *Discussion*).
