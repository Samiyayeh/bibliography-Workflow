# Strengthening Health Information Systems: Policies, Gaps, and a Framework for Reform

**Category:** [[Related Literature local]]

**Ida Marie T. Pantig, Valerie Gilbert T. Ulep, Anne Marie T. Rey, Ma. Norma Thea Madeline M. Conjares, Jann Trizia B. Talamayan, Jomelle John Anthony V. Wong, and Aliyah Lou A. Evangelista. 2025. *Strengthening Health Information Systems: Policies, Gaps, and a Framework for Reform*. PIDS Discussion Paper Series No. 2025-31. Philippine Institute for Development Studies, Quezon City, Philippines. https://doi.org/10.62986/dp2025.31**

- **Summary:** Evaluating the nationwide implementation of the Universal Health Care Act (Republic Act No. 11223) across Philippine local government units, this policy research paper presents a comprehensive reform framework identifying fragmented health data silos and establishing strategic priorities for standardizing electronic health record architectures in primary care delivery.
- **Relevance:** The policy analysis provides national institutional context for the Naga City Health Office system by demonstrating that transitioning municipal clinics to centralized, interoperable electronic medical repositories directly complies with the digital health mandates of Republic Act No. 11223 and resolves systemic record fragmentation across local government health units.
- **Source Reference(s):**
  - Executive Summary & Section 1 (Introduction), pp. 1–45.
  - Direct Quote: *"This paper integrates three studies that collectively assess the current state of the health information system (HIS) in the Philippines and identify strategic priorities to support the full realization of the Universal Health Care Act... addressing data fragmentation and establishing standard electronic record architectures."* (Executive Summary, p. 1)
