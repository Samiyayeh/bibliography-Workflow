## Annotated Analysis

**Summary:** To overcome fragmented access to specialized outpatient care across Brazil's public health system, this transdisciplinary action research study designed and deployed a distributed, modular health regulation architecture — using a decoupled Django backend and layered interoperability protocols — across three Brazilian states (Rio Grande do Norte, Espírito Santo, and Mato Grosso), demonstrating real-time cross-regional patient queue monitoring, resource allocation transparency, and scalable governance.

**Relevance:** The multi-region interoperability architecture directly validates the design goals of the proposed Naga City Health Office system, as deploying a shared digital patient queue and records layer across geographically distributed public health facilities confirms the feasibility of centralizing intake workflows within a single unified web platform. Specifically, the system's modular Django backend and decoupled frontend mirror the NCHO HIS full-stack architecture, reinforcing that a React-based frontend communicating with an Express/Node.js API layer and a centralized relational database can effectively serve multiple service counters under one coordinated system.
