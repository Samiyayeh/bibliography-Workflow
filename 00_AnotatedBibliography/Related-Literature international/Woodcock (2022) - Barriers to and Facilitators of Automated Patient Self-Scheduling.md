# Barriers to and Facilitators of Automated Patient Self-scheduling for Health Care Organizations: Scoping Review

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Elizabeth W. Woodcock. 2022. Barriers to and Facilitators of Automated Patient Self-scheduling for Health Care Organizations: Scoping Review. *Journal of Medical Internet Research* 24, 1 (January 2022), e28323. https://doi.org/10.2196/28323**

- **Summary:** Applying the Consolidated Framework for Implementation Research (CFIR) across 30 peer-reviewed full-text studies, this scoping review synthesizes the systemic determinants of automated patient self-scheduling and queue allocation platforms, establishing that real-time calendar synchronization and automated booking logic improve patient arrival rates and reduce counter administrative workloads, while technical usability and workflow alignment act as primary adoption facilitators.
- **Relevance:** The implementation science framework directly informs the patient-facing intake and self-ticketing architecture of the proposed Naga City Health Office system by identifying the operational prerequisites for successful digital scheduling adoption in outpatient environments. Specifically, the review's findings validate the NCHO HIS self-service queuing kiosk design, demonstrating that providing an intuitive, low-friction digital intake interface reduces physical counter congestion and administrative staff burden.
- **Source Reference(s):**
  - Abstract & Results, Subsection: *Intervention Characteristics and Relative Advantages*, pp. 1–9.
  - Direct Quote: *"Automated patient self-scheduling of outpatient appointments has demonstrable advantages in the form of patients’ arrival rates, labor savings, and patient satisfaction... Self-scheduling, compared with using the telephone or walk-in counters, was most often cited as a relative advantage."* (p. 2)
