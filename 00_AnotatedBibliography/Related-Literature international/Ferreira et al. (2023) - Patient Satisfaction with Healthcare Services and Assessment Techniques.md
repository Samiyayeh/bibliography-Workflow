# Patient Satisfaction with Healthcare Services and the Techniques Used for its Assessment: A Systematic Literature Review and a Bibliometric Analysis

**Category:** [[Related Literature international]]

**Diogo Cunha Ferreira, Inês Vieira, Maria Isabel Pedro, Paulo Caldas, and Miguel Varela. 2023. Patient Satisfaction with Healthcare Services and the Techniques Used for its Assessment: A Systematic Literature Review and a Bibliometric Analysis. *Healthcare* 11, 5 (February 2023), 639. https://doi.org/10.3390/healthcare11050639**

- **Summary:** Combining a systematic literature review with bibliometric mapping across international clinical datasets, this study establishes a theoretical taxonomy of healthcare service quality dimensions, demonstrating that physical waiting time operates as a primary baseline determinant where unmanaged delays disproportionately drive patient dissatisfaction and perceived barriers to medical care.
- **Relevance:** The theoretical satisfaction model establishes foundational evaluation criteria for the proposed Naga City Health Office system by demonstrating that minimizing intake and consultation waiting intervals is the single most critical operational factor influencing patient perception of public healthcare quality. Specifically, these findings justify the NCHO HIS automated queuing engine and real-time display architecture, confirming that providing transparent queue progression directly mitigates the psychological and operational friction of clinical waiting times.
- **Source Reference(s):**
  - Section: *Dimensions and Determinants of Patient Satisfaction*, Subsection 3.2, pp. 6–12.
  - Direct Quote: *"Waiting time is consistently identified as a critical must-have requirement in outpatient services, where prolonged delays significantly increase patient dissatisfaction and represent a substantial barrier to accessible care."* (p. 8)
