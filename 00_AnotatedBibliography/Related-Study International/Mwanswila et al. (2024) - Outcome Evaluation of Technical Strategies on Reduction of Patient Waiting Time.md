# Outcome Evaluation of Technical Strategies on Reduction of Patient Waiting Time in the Outpatient Department at Kilimanjaro Christian Medical Centre—Northern Tanzania

**Category:** [[Related Study International]]

**ACM Citation:**
Mwanswila, M. J., Mollel, H. A., and Mushi, L. D. 2024. Outcome evaluation of technical strategies on reduction of patient waiting time in the outpatient department at Kilimanjaro Christian Medical Centre—Northern Tanzania. *BMC Health Services Research* 24, 1 (2024), 785. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11231-5
	
- **Summary:**  
  Mwanswila et al. (2024) evaluated technical interventions in a high-volume outpatient department using an analytical cross-sectional design, establishing that transitioning to an Electronic Medical Record (EMR) system served as a primary predictor for a 45% reduction in median patient turnaround and waiting times across registration, triage, and pharmacy service points.

- **Relevance:**  
  The investigation demonstrates high statistical validity by analyzing multi-year administrative and timestamped operational records within an active public healthcare setting. Published in *BMC Health Services Research*, the quantitative findings confirm that digitizing patient intake workflows significantly mitigates departmental service bottlenecks. However, the study identifies that technical deployment without continuous staff training can create localized friction during initial consultations. These empirical findings justify the architectural logic of integrating a centralized Electronic Health Record (EHR) database with an automated queuing module for the Naga City Health Office to replace paper-based record retrievals. Specifically, the authors' verification of accelerated registration and triage timestamps directly supports the proposed system's module routing design, aligning with ISO/IEC 25010 performance efficiency standards for decreasing patient wait times.
