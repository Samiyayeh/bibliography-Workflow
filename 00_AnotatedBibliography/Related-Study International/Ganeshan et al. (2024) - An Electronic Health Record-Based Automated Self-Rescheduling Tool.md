# An Electronic Health Record–Based Automated Self-Rescheduling Tool to Improve Patient Access: Retrospective Cohort Study

**Category:** [[Related Study International]]

**Smitha Ganeshan, Andrew W. Liu, Anne Kroeger, Prerna Anand, Richard Seefeldt, Alexis Regner, Diana Vaughn, Anobel Y. Odisho, and Michelle Mourad. 2024. An Electronic Health Record–Based Automated Self-Rescheduling Tool to Improve Patient Access: Retrospective Cohort Study. *Journal of Medical Internet Research* 26 (2024), e52071. https://doi.org/10.2196/52071**

- **Summary:** To address extended clinic appointment delays and inefficient slot utilization resulting from cancellations, this retrospective cohort study evaluated 60,660 automated scheduling offers managed within an Electronic Health Record environment, demonstrating that automated queue re-allocation enabled patients to receive clinical care a median of 14 days earlier while recapturing 2,576 service hours.
- **Relevance:** The empirical results substantiate the automated queuing and scheduling logic of the proposed Naga City Health Office system, as the documented 14-day reduction in consultation delays and 2,576 recaptured operational hours indicate that linking dynamic queue assignment directly to centralized electronic records maximizes provider capacity and reduces patient backlogs.

  - Direct Quote: *"Patients were seen a median (IQR) of 14 (4-33) days sooner for their appointments... Fast Pass added 2576 patient service hours to the clinical schedule, with a median (IQR) of 251 (216-322) hours per month."* (Abstract)
