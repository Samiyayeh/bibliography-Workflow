# Physician-Customized Strategies for Reducing Outpatient Waiting Time in South Korea Using Queueing Theory and Probabilistic Metamodels

**Category:** [[Related Study International]]

**ACM Citation:**
Lee, H., Choi, E. K., Min, K. A., Bae, E., Lee, H., and Lee, J.-S. 2022. Physician-Customized Strategies for Reducing Outpatient Waiting Time in South Korea Using Queueing Theory and Probabilistic Metamodels. *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health* 19, 4 (2022), 2073. https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph19042073

- **Summary:**  
  Lee et al. (2022) formulated an optimization framework using queueing theory, probabilistic metamodels, and a non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm (NSGA-II) to analyze outpatient bottleneck dynamics, demonstrating an average 30% reduction in the probability of patients experiencing extended consultation wait times.

- **Relevance:**  
  The study evaluates operational parameters using physician-level empirical datasets and multi-objective evolutionary computation, providing methodological rigor when modeling non-linear outpatient arrival patterns. Published in the peer-reviewed *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*, the findings provide credible mathematical verification of queue optimization. However, the algorithmic model assumes digital scheduling infrastructure and physician adherence to allocated consultation intervals, which requires adaptation when applied to high-volume, unscheduled walk-in public health environments. These theoretical findings justify the core logic of automating patient queue assignment within the Naga City Health Office to balance physician workloads. Specifically, the metamodel approach supports the proposed system's module routing design, directly informing ISO/IEC 25010 performance efficiency benchmarks by minimizing turnaround delays between queue ticket issuance and clinical examination.
