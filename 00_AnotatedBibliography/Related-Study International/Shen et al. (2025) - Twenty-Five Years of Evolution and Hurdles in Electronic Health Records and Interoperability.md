# Twenty-Five Years of Evolution and Hurdles in Electronic Health Records and Interoperability in Medical Research: Comprehensive Review

**Category:** [[Related Study International]]

**ACM Citation:**
Shen, Y., Yu, J., Zhou, J., and Hu, G. 2025. Twenty-Five Years of Evolution and Hurdles in Electronic Health Records and Interoperability in Medical Research: Comprehensive Review. *Journal of Medical Internet Research* 27 (2025), e59024. https://doi.org/10.2196/59024

- **Summary:**  
  Shen et al. (2025) conducted a comprehensive review of electronic health record (EHR) systems spanning two and a half decades, demonstrating that standardized interoperability architectures and centralized database consolidation are essential prerequisites for eliminating fragmented patient records and enabling real-time clinical data exchange.

- **Relevance:**  
  The investigation provides high academic credibility by evaluating twenty-five years of indexed peer-reviewed literature across major biomedical repositories to identify technical and semantic data integration patterns. Published in the *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, the paper presents a rigorous synthesis of structural interoperability barriers and database standardization models. However, the analysis primarily synthesizes high-level informatics frameworks, leaving localized network constraints and hardware resource limits in municipal public health clinics less explored. These theoretical findings justify the architectural logic of establishing direct cross-module interoperability within the proposed Naga City Health Office system. Specifically, the authors' analysis of data fragmentation confirms that linking queue identification numbers directly to centralized patient records prevents redundant documentation and accelerates clinical file retrieval, supporting ISO/IEC 25010 performance efficiency and functional suitability objectives.
