# Standards, Processes, and Tools Used to Evaluate the Quality of Health Information Systems: Systematic Literature Review

**Category:** [[Related Study International]]

**ACM Citation:**
Noël, R., Taramasco, C., and Márquez, G. 2022. Standards, Processes, and Tools Used to Evaluate the Quality of Health Information Systems: Systematic Literature Review. *Journal of Medical Internet Research* 24, 3 (2022), e26577. https://doi.org/10.2196/26577

- **Summary:**  
  Noël et al. (2022) conducted a systematic literature review analyzing evaluation standards for Health Information Systems (HIS), establishing that ISO/IEC 25010 serves as the primary benchmark for assessing software product quality, specifically across usability, performance efficiency, data security, and system compatibility.

- **Relevance:**  
  The study demonstrates high methodological rigor by systematically screening major scientific repositories according to PRISMA guidelines to evaluate international quality frameworks. Published in the *Journal of Medical Internet Research*, the research provides credible analytical evidence on how software quality characteristics are measured in healthcare contexts. However, the authors note that empirical studies often prioritize technical functional compliance over longitudinal assessments of clinician usability and operational response times in resource-constrained environments. These theoretical findings justify adopting the ISO/IEC 25010 standard to evaluate the proposed Naga City Health Office system. Specifically, the authors' categorization of performance efficiency and usability characteristics supports the evaluation metrics defined for the project, validating the need to systematically measure queue processing speeds and healthcare worker adoption rates.
