# Criteria-based outpatient scheduling at a nephrology clinic: prospective evaluation of patient pre-assessment and its corresponding adaptive scheduling strategy

**Category:** [[Related Study International]]

**Ruben Klaas, Jedidja Lok-Visser, Joan Doornebal, Ton Roelofs, Sebastian Rachuba, and Gréanne Leeftink. 2024. Criteria-based outpatient scheduling at a nephrology clinic: prospective evaluation of patient pre-assessment and its corresponding adaptive scheduling strategy. *BMC Health Services Research* 24, 1 (2024), 1145. https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-024-11615-7**

- **Summary:** To address outpatient clinic congestion and provider shortages caused by unvetted routine consultations, this prospective mixed-methods study combined a 12-month retrospective cohort analysis of 4,861 visits with stochastic mathematical modeling and computer simulation, demonstrating that criteria-based patient pre-assessment reduced unnecessary consultations by 18% and enabled clinical operations to absorb a 20% surge in patient demand without degrading schedule performance.
- **Relevance:** The empirical findings validate the queuing and intake triage logic of the proposed Naga City Health Office system, as the 18% reduction in unnecessary consultation load and the 20% expansion in patient capacity confirm that embedding criteria-based intake filtering directly into digital queue routing maximizes physician utilization and prevents outpatient department overcrowding.
- **Source Reference(s):**
  - Abstract & Section "Results" (pp. 1–12).
  - Direct Quote: *"875 (18%) of the realized appointments in 2022 did not meet the OC visit pre-assessment criteria... Using the pre-assessment strategy combined with the best performing scheduling strategy under full capacity (108 slots), up to 20% increase in patient demand can be handled with equal operational performance."* (Abstract, p. 1)
